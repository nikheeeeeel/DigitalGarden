The rains will come.

From MAKE-A-TON 7.0 to leading TinkerHub at FISAT.

This story started 2 years back, October 2024, one of my first hackathons, MAKE-A-TON 7.0. For a second year engineering student with even a hint of interest in hackathons, this would have been a very big deal, and it was. I was over the moon but also I knew my GitHub was as dry as my experiences in hackathons so they probably took us in because of my emotionally convincing story that I wrote in the application form. Nonetheless this is where it all started, at CUSAT, a college I admired but lacked the academic gift to get into.

Make-a-ton was an experience to say the least. A cultural shock in itself. This is where I found out good hackathons are supposed to be free.

Now get into my shoes for a second here. At CUSAT, surrounded by very sharp minds from all over India, in a hackathon where 7000+ students applied and somehow three of us from FISAT got in and we are very well aware it's not because of our skills, at least not the one that matters here. You would be intimidated. You would feel weak. I did, until I didn't.

It was a struggle I agree but we were really stubborn people. We thought we stood a chance. That night saw conversations that sounded like board room discussions. We were so delusional that we promised we would not let this go after this hackathon. We would build this and we would get this thing done. That never happened but what did happen was three students from FISAT understood their dreams are not smaller than the others in that room. That is the power of INFLUENCE.

When you are in these rooms it would be surprising to not hear someone talk about TinkerHub. Fast forward a few weeks later, my hackathon partner, Noyal and I found ourselves standing infront of TinkerSpace. Both of us not vouched, both of us motivating the other to go in first. That went on for some time until we talked about it long enough that it made us feel way too stupid so we just went in. We saw a guy, talked, he vouched us and we were in. It was that easy. I don't even remember if we worked on anything that day. We just felt like we were on mars. We left that room with probably the biggest smile in the world. I cannot explain the feeling of finding a space where you leave all the academic doubt behind and you just feel like yourself again.

You would think one of us would have applied for the campus community the second we got out but no, we didn't think we were good enough at that point, in retrospect that was a very bad decision that ended up being the right decision. I am currently in my fourth year and I have had my fair share of experience hosting, organizing and participating in events in the past two years that I finally felt ready. Coupled with the fact that a graduating lead from another campus had called me asking if I wanted to apply, call it, kiss of fate.

Noyal pushed me to apply and I did, I failed in shaking his resolve to not apply so it was just me from my campus. Going into the selection camp I felt the feeling of not belonging coming in waves, pushing it away each time, I got through it. Then came the news that I had got in. I was happy but amma and chechi were happier. They knew how much I wanted this.

That reminds me, why do I want this so bad? Well, ever since first year I have been consistently average in studying. Again, that sense of not belonging. Funny thing is in my 4 years the only place I felt like I belonged was in that room at Make-a-ton 7.0. At a college that would chew me up and spit me out academically, where I forgot for a few hours about the useless tags we are all assigned by ourselves and our peers and faculty, and I fully immersed myself into making something. That's where I felt like myself. After that I have been obsessing about the fact that influence is the single biggest thing that shapes all of us. I felt like that influence knows no age group, no cgpa, no tiers and no high packages. It's something much more trivial than that but with the power to make you feel like you belong in any summit that looks too high to climb. I wanted everyone in my campus to have an opportunity to feel that same way. Simply because of how liberating that feels.

From when I applied to become lead till that graduation day, that Tinkerhub did for their past leads, I created memories I would cherish the most in my journey, with people like and unlike me all there for a similar cause. The graduation day in particular was my full circle moment. Almost half of the people that organized, volunteered, mentored and judged in makeaton 7.0 were in that graduation auditorium. I saw the same documentary of Meher and Abid that me and Noyal saw at the space carnival, for the first time, a few years back. That same feeling that I struggled with was no more. I felt like I belonged but more importantly I felt like I have an opportunity to make more people in my college feel that sort of way.

This is my story that started years ago. This space gave me a voice to share it and I hope when building this community at FISAT it gives voices to more stories. Stories that prove the difference the right influence can make on students.

A space where engineering students in Kerala dreams the same dreams as someone studying at MIT (not Manipal). Sounds delusional, everything does in the beginning. This is one of those beginnings :)
